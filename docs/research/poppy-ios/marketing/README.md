## What these two screens accomplish

### Slide 1 — the problem (`01`)

Eight stacked iOS notifications from Resy, WhatsApp, Maps, Uber, Instagram, Google Calendar, Waymo, Messages. Deliberate dramaturgy:

- Out-of-order timestamps (visually chaotic)
- Oversized badge counts (123, 67, 23, 15) — feels suffocating
- Mix of social, transit, food, productivity, news — represents the full attention surface area
- Pitch-black background — notifications glow against void

The slide doesn't say "we solve fragmentation." It STAGES the fragmentation. The user sees their own daily chaos in cartoonish-but-recognizable form.

### Slide 2 — the solution (`02`)

Two icons on a black field:

```
        [📱 iOS Messages icon]   ← top, smaller


              [🌅 Poppy app icon] ← bottom, larger
              "Meet Poppy"
```

Two crucial composition choices:

1. **Messages icon ABOVE Poppy** — visually establishes that Messages is the surface, Poppy is the new tenant in it. The marketing IS the positioning.
2. **Poppy icon is a sunrise behind a poppy flower** — metaphor for "ahead of the day" / new beginning / morning. The product's emotional promise embedded in the icon.

The pairing tells the user: **"You don't install another app to use. You install something that lives in the messaging app you already use."**

## Voice / aesthetic

- **Pitch-black background** dominates both slides — establishes brand as serious, focused, calm
- **Orange poppy** is the only warm color — the agent itself is the warmth in the cold attention landscape
- **No text on Slide 1** — the visual IS the message
- **Two-word title on Slide 2** ("Meet Poppy") — minimal copy
- **iOS-native rendering** — both slides look like iOS screens, not marketing illustrations. Lowers the perceived gap between "the marketing" and "the product."

## Steal for CareSupport

1. **Visual positioning over copy** — App Store screenshots should STAGE the problem, not describe it. For CareSupport: a slide showing fragmented family-text chaos ("Mom didn't take her pill — Helper Mei", "Dad's appointment is moved — Coordinator Jen", "Can someone pick up groceries — Sister Ana") — the actual texts caregivers exchange daily, rendered as overlapping iMessage threads.

2. **Pair messaging icon with agent icon** — In our App Store preview slide 2, show iMessage icon + CareSupport icon. Visual signal: "lives where you already are." Don't show our app's home screen first.

3. **Agent icon embeds emotional promise** — Poppy's sunrise+flower says "morning, fresh start." CareSupport's icon should embed an emotion appropriate to caregiving — calm, presence, gentle vigilance. Avoid medical clichés (red cross, stethoscope) — they signal sickness, not support.

4. **Restrained color palette** — Mostly dark/neutral, one warm accent for the agent. Don't try to be friendly with rainbow gradients; trust comes from restraint.

5. **iOS-native rendering** — Marketing slides should look like the actual product, not glossy renders. Caregivers should see "this looks like my iMessage" and trust that we belong there.

6. **Two-slide test** — Can we communicate the entire positioning in TWO App Store images? If not, we don't know our positioning well enough. Iterate until we can.

# Doctrine Articulations — The Full Weight

This is the long-form record. `DOCTRINE.md` is the law; this is the legislative history. It preserves how the theses were reached, in the language they were reached in, so that future readers — cofounders, investors, judges, engineers — can audit the reasoning and not just inherit the conclusions.

Provenance: founder–Cascade working sessions, June 11–12, 2026, building on the June 7 founder–Obssa product-vision call and the June 9 Clinton handoff call.

---

## I. The Interview Record

The founder's instruction: *"I think about the end game and inverse backwards."* The interview therefore started at the terminal state and walked toward the present.

### Q1 — What does CareSupport terminally become?

The fork: (a) become a licensed FMS — payroll, FICA, workers' comp, the DHS contract, the full per-member-per-month fee; (b) the interface layer — families and FMSs run on it, the licensed layer keeps the money and the liability; (c) the protocol — monetize the channel.

**Resolution: (b), pragmatically and without drama.** In the founder's words: *"If TurboTax's entire genius was refusing to touch the money — no payroll, no liability, no license — I think that makes a lot of sense for CareSupport."* No need to delete the FMS or to dream of Free-File-style non-compete agreements. The aim is a refinement where the plan is fundamentally possible and any stakeholder who sees it can validate it.

Later refinement (see §IV–V): "interface, not money" is a **stage posture, not a permanent identity**. Vision is abstract and evolving; earlier words are waypoints, not walls. The completed entity is architected so the funds flow is an option to exercise, never a prerequisite.

### Q2 — What is the single artifact CareSupport produces first?

"Documentation" is abstract; TurboTax's artifact was concrete (a completed 1040, formatted to IRS schema, transmitted). The care equivalent must be (1) something the FMS already requires today, (2) high-volume and error-prone, (3) plausibly derivable from coordination texts.

Candidates: time entries / visit records; care plans; budget tracking.

**Resolution: the payment-survival bundle — time entries + daily care notes** — sharpened by the Givers research (§III): enrollment is owned and one-shot; care plans are annual and clinical; but the recurring documentation that keeps payments flowing is unowned, daily, and exactly what coordination exhaust contains.

The falsifiable sentence: *"Enrolled families lose payments over bad paperwork; CareSupport turns the texting they already do into the daily notes and time entries that keep them paid."* An FMS operations manager can confirm or kill this in one conversation. Every claim in the doctrine must meet this standard: checkable by the stakeholder who would know.

## II. TurboTax — The Accurate History

The founder's instruction: *"This is not the time to be imaginative. We need to be accurate."* Researched June 11, 2026.

**The positioning was not "we'll help you build a product." It was a non-compete with the state.**

- **2002 — the Free File deal.** The IRS, under Bush-era e-government pressure, was preparing to build its own free online filing system. The industry — organized largely by Intuit and guided by a former Intuit lobbyist — countered with the *Free Online Electronic Tax Filing Agreement* (October 2002): private companies provide free filing to ~70% of taxpayers through their own products, and **in exchange the IRS contractually agreed not to develop its own filing portal** (Free File Alliance, Wikipedia; ProPublica).
- **The state kept the rails; Intuit took the interface.** The IRS owns e-file/MeF — schemas, submission infrastructure, "authorized e-file provider" status. TurboTax owns the consumer relationship and formats/transmits clean structured data into government rails. **TurboTax never touches the money.** No refunds flow through it, no tax liability — data formatting, transmission, and fees only.
- **The moat was enforcement, not technology.** California's ReadyReturn (2005) pre-filled returns for citizens; Intuit spent $1.7M–$3M+ lobbying to kill it (LA Times; ProPublica; CalFile, Wikipedia). When the IRS finally built **Direct File** (2024–25, praised by users in 25 states), the next administration killed it — the IRS confirmed in November 2025 that it would not exist for the 2026 season (Federal News Network; AP). 296,000 users one year; zero the next (Fortune, April 2026).
- **The end state, by the numbers:** ~60% US market share (Citi 2025); Intuit Consumer Group revenue $4.9B in FY2025; the growth engine is **TurboTax Live** — humans attached to software — up 47%, already 41% of consumer revenue (Intuit investor release). Cost of the playbook: an FTC order against deceptive "free" advertising (January 2024).

**Mapped honestly onto care:**

| Tax world | Care world |
|---|---|
| IRS (rails: e-file/MeF) | Medicaid / DHS (rails: EVV, claims) |
| Taxpayer | Self-directing family |
| Tax preparer (displaced by TurboTax) | FMS processing function (made obsolete by clean data) |
| TurboTax (interface; never touches money) | CareSupport |
| TurboTax Live (humans; 41% of consumer revenue) | Assisted care coordination, later |

**The two political lessons cut both ways:** the deal is the moat (Intuit's durable asset was a signed agreement that the state won't compete, defended for twenty years) — and the deal is mortal (Direct File got built the moment one administration funded it; it died when another defunded it). The care equivalent of the threat: DHS procuring its own consumer-grade documentation interface. The care equivalent of the opportunity: being the standing infrastructure the state has no incentive to duplicate.

**The lesson refused:** TurboTax's moat depended on keeping filing painful. Care verification is broken enough that CareSupport wins by *fixing* it — the anti-fraud layer is politically protected in a way the keep-it-complicated layer never is.

## III. Givers — The Accurate Model and the Daily-Layer Hole

Researched June 11, 2026, at the founder's direction ("Go to givers.com and research them"). Source: givers.com; founder Max Mayblum on the AgeTech Podcast.

**What Givers actually is:**

- **Front end — navigation.** They mapped every caregiver-payment program in the US into a database. Free TurboTax-style screener (Mayblum's words: *"a TurboTax type of solution"*) → personalized program-eligibility recommendation. Massive state-by-state SEO engine ("how to get paid as a caregiver in [state]" × 50). Millions of people per year through their content.
- **Back end — they touch the money.** Direct quote: *"In some states we actually have licensure that we can be the provider of choice... we'll actually be their agency of record... and then we also reimburse them through Medicaid."* They became the **licensed home care agency, state by state**, with the training/coaching obligations that come with it.
- **The economics under that:** in Structured Family Caregiving programs, Medicaid pays the managing agency a daily stipend; **50–65% goes to the caregiver, the agency keeps the rest** (Givers' own SFC explainer). Their SFC footprint is the one-live-in-caregiver, daily-stipend model — *not* the multi-caregiver self-direction model (CDCS/CFSS + FMS) that Rob lives in.

**What Givers proves, and the hole it leaves:**

- Proven: demand at the front door — "how do I get paid to care" pulls millions a year. Navigation is a validated acquisition wedge; it is also owned, and one-time per family.
- Proven: the documentation dependency — their own copy says ongoing payment depends on daily notes and care logs.
- Proven: someone must hold the license — in SFC states it's Givers; in self-direction it's the FMS. Either way the money-toucher exists; partner with that layer.
- **The hole: nobody owns the daily layer.** Enrollment is an event. Care is every day. Once a family is enrolled — by Givers, an FMS, or a county worker — the operational reality (twelve caregivers, shift swaps, coverage gaps, and the documentation stream payments depend on) has no product. That is where coordination exhaust becomes the artifact, and where CareSupport lives.

Givers is a future channel partner (they enroll; CareSupport operates), not a competitor — unless CareSupport mistakenly fights them for the front door instead of owning the house.

## IV. Stripe — The Structural Reading and the Completed Entity

The founder: *"CareSupport literally is the industry's, so to speak, Stripe. If you pull the regulations, the bureaucracy, the documents, and the requirements, it's all connected to the financing — where money goes."*

**What Stripe actually is, so the analogy carries weight:** Stripe's insight wasn't "payments." It was that **compliance and money movement are the same system, so whoever abstracts the compliance owns the flow.** Behind "seven lines of code," Stripe absorbed acquiring banks, card-network rules, PCI, KYC, fraud — and critically, it **did not become a bank**. It partnered with the licensed layer and monetized the volume passing through. Then it compounded: Radar, Treasury, Issuing — each layer making departure more impossible.

The founder's observation is the same insight transposed: pull on any regulation, document, or requirement in care, and the thread ends at financing. **Care documentation is not paperwork — it is the release mechanism for money.** Medicaid moved ~$909B in FY2024; HCBS is north of $100B of it; self-direction is ~1.5M people and growing. Every dollar is gated on proof that care happened, and the proof layer is garbage: punch-clock EVV apps, paper timesheets, fax machines, fraud scandals.

**CareSupport, the completed entity: the system of record for whether care actually happened — the verification rails every money-toucher in the care economy depends on.**

The stack:

- **L1 — The thread** (acquisition + ground truth). Families coordinate in text because that is where care already lives. Free forever. The unfakeable asset: ground truth captured at the moment of coordination — who confirmed the shift, who showed up, what was done, corroborated across twelve people's messages. Richer than any clock-punch.
- **L2 — The coordination engine.** Conversation → structured operational state. Already in embryo: `careContacts`, `coordinationEvents`, `outreachAttempts`, `careClaims`, `auditLogs` in the Convex schema. The rails' data model was being built before it was named.
- **L3 — The verified care record.** The atomic unit of the company. Time + presence + task + consent + audit trail, derived from L2, held to evidentiary standard. To CareSupport what the charge object is to Stripe.
- **L4 — Program schemas (regulation as code).** CFSS-Minnesota is schema #1. Then program by program, state by state — the operational twin of Givers' eligibility database. Tedious, unglamorous, compounding: exactly why nobody does it.
- **L5 — The API (UCP's real meaning).** FMSs, agencies, MCOs, enrollers, eventually DHS — they don't get a dashboard; they get verified care records in their required format. The Universal Care Protocol is not a standards crusade; it is the schema your records arrive in becoming the de facto standard because everyone is already consuming it. That is how Stripe became a "standard": adoption, not committee.

**Revenue at completion:** infrastructure pricing on the money-touchers — per-member-per-month plus per-verified-record, the way Stripe takes its slice of every charge. The family never pays. The state never pays directly. The licensed intermediaries pay because the cost of proof has been deleted — and because their auditors trust these records more than their own.

**The fork the word "Stripe" forces:** Stripe sits in the funds flow. Two honest versions of the completed entity:

- **Plaid-of-care:** verification/data rails only. Money never passes through. Cleaner, faster, smaller terminal value.
- **Stripe-of-care:** the reimbursement itself eventually clears over the rails — via partner institutions holding the licenses, exactly as Stripe rode banks. Bigger terminal value, and it does not violate the never-be-the-bank principle: Stripe never became a bank; it became the interface to banks.

**Resolution: build Plaid-of-care, architect for Stripe-of-care.** Every verified care record is structured so a payment instruction can one day hang off it. The option is earned, not pitched. Stripe didn't pitch Treasury in 2011 either.

## V. The Vector of Frames and the Terminal Frame

The founder's instruction: vision is abstract and conceptual; prior words are waypoints, not walls. The task: project the vector, not the words.

**The vector — each frame swallowing the last:**

1. *An app that remembers things* → swallowed by
2. *A wedge into one trusted thread* → swallowed by
3. *Coordination is documentation* → swallowed by
4. *Documentation is reimbursement (TurboTax)* → swallowed by
5. *Nobody owns the daily layer* → swallowed by
6. *It's all connected to where money goes (Stripe)*

The pattern in the expansion: every frame moves CareSupport **deeper beneath the industry** — from product, to pipe, to rails. Extrapolated one frame further:

**The terminal frame: care is the largest economic activity in America that has never been economically legible.**

AARP estimates unpaid family care at roughly $600B a year — work that exists in no ledger, builds no work history, earns no credit, appears in no labor market, and is invisible to every system that allocates money. The paid side — $100B+ of HCBS — is barely more legible: it runs on rails borrowed from clinics and punch-clocks, which is why it leaks fraud and drowns in paper.

The completed CareSupport is not a coordination app, not a documentation pipe, not even payment rails. It is **the infrastructure that makes an act of care a first-class economic event** — verifiable, payable, auditable, insurable, creditable — independent of who the payer happens to be.

From that altitude, everything reorders:

- **Medicaid isn't the market. It's the first payer** — the one currently desperate enough (fraud, deficits, silver tsunami) to pay for legibility.
- **The FMS isn't the customer. It's the first consumer of verified care records.** MCOs, the VA, LTC insurers, employer caregiver benefits, and payers that don't exist yet are the rest of the queue.
- **CFSS isn't the program. It's schema #1** in the library that codifies care regulation as software.
- **The thread isn't the product. It's the sensor** — the only instrument positioned where care actually happens, which is why the legibility is trustworthy at all.
- **Rob isn't the user. He's the existence proof** — twelve people's invisible labor made visible, verified, and economically real.

And things become visible from here that the lower frames could not see: a caregiver who has cared for a parent for six years currently exits with *nothing on paper* — no employment record, no credit signal, nothing. Verified care records give caregivers an **economic identity**. That is not a feature; that is a constituency. The day caregivers' verified histories matter for credit, hiring, or benefits, CareSupport stops being infrastructure someone could replace and becomes infrastructure people would *defend*.

**What dissolves at this altitude:** the Plaid-vs-Stripe fork — data rails vs funds rails — dissolves into sequencing. Value follows legibility; once the substrate defines what a unit of care *is*, the money crossing it is an implementation detail decided when leverage permits. "Don't touch the money" was never a principle. It was a posture for a stage.

**The principal question:** every infrastructure company at completion has one constituency it actually serves, with everyone else paying for proximity. Stripe serves the merchant. Visa serves the bank. CareSupport's answer, already encoded in `SOUL.md` (Rob's nose; the family thread; the care recipient as the ultimate measure): **the family is the principal — states, payers, and intermediaries pay for access to the legibility the family generates.** That sentence unifies the consumer product, the B2G money, and the infrastructure ambition into one company instead of three.

## VI. The Naming — Full Articulation

The founder: *"I believe families are invisible home care agencies. And everyone knows this, but has not named it as such."*

**Why this naming is the asset:** category-creating language converts a known-but-unnamed fact into a market. Everyone in the industry experiences this — the FMS processing a mom's timesheets, the case manager calling the daughter for status, DHS paying $35/hr to visible agencies while invisible ones do identical work for $15 or free. Nobody had collapsed it into a noun. "Gig economy" did it for odd jobs. "Creator economy" did it for posting online. The naming *is* the act of making the invisible legible — the product thesis and the language are the same move.

**It is structurally true, not metaphorically.** The agency function checklist against Rob: intake & assessment (he knows the care needs better than any RN assessor); staffing & recruiting (he sourced and retains twelve caregivers); scheduling & dispatch (daily, by nose); training & supervision (he onboards every new caregiver); payroll & billing (timesheets, FMS paperwork, budget tracking); compliance & documentation (whatever the program demands); quality assurance & incident response (24/7, no off-shift). That is a home care agency's entire org chart, operating unincorporated, unpaid, and unsupported, in one man's iPhone. **Rob is not the user. Rob is the existence proof of the category.**

**What it unifies:** the thread = the invisible agency's front office; the coordination engine = its operations department; the verified care record = its billing department; the FMS/Medicaid = the back office it never had; the rails frame = infrastructure for millions of these agencies; UCP = the network they form once visible; the $35→$15 arbitrage = the value released when the invisible agency gets recognized.

One sentence for capital: *"America's largest home care provider is the family — it has just never been recognized as one. CareSupport is the infrastructure that makes the invisible agency visible, supported, and payable."*

**Two sharpenings:**

1. **Never say it to families.** This is capital/policy/judge language — it converts investors and states because it reframes supply. But a daughter drowning in her mother's care does not want to hear she's running an agency; that adds identity burden to labor burden. The TurboTax move applies: agency-grade capability without agency-grade identity. `SOUL.md` already enforces the family-facing voice: fewer times Rob uses his nose. **Category language up; burden-relief language down.**
2. **"Visible" must never mean "licensed."** The thesis dies if it implies families should become agencies — licensure, liability, surveys. The claim is the opposite: the agency function exists; CareSupport supplies the institutional shell as software so the family never has to incorporate the burden.

## VII. The Dimensional Space — Design Law, Policy Bet, Democratization

The founder: *"The industry fundamentally has built home care software for decades now and has essentially designed it completely for enterprise, for B2B. If families are invisible home care agencies, this means we need to design, engineer, and build home care software for families... We live in the present, but the vision is in the future. We are betting on policy to change — that in 20 years it is the given that families who are invisible home care agencies and who are entitled to Medicaid would ultimately benefit greatly by self-serving their home care needs, which is what the democratization of past industries has done."*

**The design law:** home care software has existed for forty years and has never once been built for the entity that delivers most of the care. WellSky, Axxess, AlayaCare, HHAeXchange — all of it encodes the *agency*: rosters, billing portals, compliance dashboards. Enterprise software always encodes its buyer, and the family was never the buyer — so the family got nothing. The largest provider class in the industry has zero software designed for it.

**The design descent was the research:** SaaS web app → iOS app → iMessage is not product indecision — it is a discovery procedure, each step removing "software" until only the family's native medium remained. Enterprise software encodes org charts; families don't have org charts, they have group texts. Family-grade home care software, taken to its logical conclusion, *does not look like software at all*. The agency gets a dashboard. The family gets a text thread that happens to run an agency behind it. Poke/Poppy are the form-factor proof that consumers accept an agent living in iMessage; the repo is the MVP proving the care version of that form factor — a conceptual MVP, exactly as scoped, whose data model is the embryo of the rails.

**The bet, stated precisely — it is not "policy might change"; it is policy has no choice.** The arithmetic is deterministic:

- The 85+ population roughly doubles by the 2040s. Peak care demand has a date on it.
- The direct-care workforce already cannot staff today's demand — the projected shortfall is millions of workers. There is no version of 2035 where agencies at $35/hr scale to meet it. The workers don't exist and the money doesn't exist.
- Medicaid is being cut, not grown. **Austerity does not threaten self-direction — austerity is its engine**, because families at $15/hr are the only supply that scales *and* saves money simultaneously.
- The direction is already in motion: self-direction enrollment grows every census cycle (~1.5M and climbing); COVID's Appendix K made paying family caregivers normal in nearly every state and much of it stuck; Minnesota launched CFSS in late 2024 — the founder's own state expanding budget authority right now.

This is the same class of bet as Tesla on EVs in 2010 or SpaceX on commercial procurement in 2008: the incumbent model (Boeing, in the founder's own analogy) is arithmetically unsustainable, and the government flips not from idealism but from having no alternative.

**The democratization pattern, formalized — every industry in the analogy set followed four phases:**

1. **Capability locked in enterprises** — fleets, hotels, brokerages, tax preparers, *home care agencies*
2. **Infrastructure makes the individual capable** — smartphone+GPS, Airbnb, Robinhood, TurboTax, **CareSupport**
3. **Policy legitimizes the informal actor** — rideshare law, STR permits, e-file, **self-direction expansion** ← *we are here*
4. **The individual becomes default supply; incumbents retreat to the complex edge** — agencies keep ventilator-dependent and post-acute care; families do the rest

**The analogy set resolves into components of one machine, not competing metaphors:** Givers — proof of demand at the front door (and proof of the agency-of-record trap to avoid); TurboTax — the compliance translation engine, regulation as code riding government rails; Plaid — the position, verification/data connectivity that money-touchers depend on; Stripe — the terminal state, the infrastructure all value in the category flows across; Poke — the form factor, the agent living where the family already lives; Uber/Airbnb/SpaceX — the survival playbook for the policy gap.

**How a policy-bet company survives the gap:** every stage must be valuable under today's rules, so no one ever has to believe the endgame. Tesla sold Roadsters before the EV mandates. The CareSupport ladder: the thread relieves Rob *today* (no policy needed); time-entry exhaust saves an FMS processing cost *today* (current rules); the CFSS schema monetizes *today's* program. The bet doesn't gate the company — it compounds it. If policy flips in eight years instead of twenty, CareSupport is the standing infrastructure. If it flips slowly, CareSupport is a profitable translation layer the entire way.

**The complete dimensional space, in one line each:** a **design thesis** (family-grade care software = no software), a **category thesis** (families are invisible home care agencies), an **economic thesis** (legibility releases the $35→$15 arbitrage), and a **policy thesis** (demographics force the flip) — four layers, one company.

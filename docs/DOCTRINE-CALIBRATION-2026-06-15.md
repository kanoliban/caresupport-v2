# CareSupport Doctrine Calibration — June 15, 2026

This document preserves the precise calibration from the June 15, 2026 shared-understanding conversation. The founder articulated the company in their own words; this document records where that articulation is correct, where it should be sharpened, and the most defensible version of the thesis.

---

## Summary

The founder is **not wrong in the core shape**. The articulation is probably the cleanest statement of the company so far.

The corrections are not to the thesis. They are to the **precision of a few claims** that could otherwise create unnecessary attack surfaces with investors, payers, states, FMS providers, or technical stakeholders.

---

## Where the Founder Is Right

### 1. The starting observation is a naming problem

Yes.

The category creation begins with:

> **Families are invisible home care agencies.**

That naming converts a known reality into an addressable market. Families already do agency-level operations, but because the work is socially framed as “just family,” it has no infrastructure, no ledger, no reimbursement pathway, no operating system.

That is the founding insight.

### 2. CareSupport instruments an existing workflow

Yes.

This is essential. CareSupport does **not** ask families to adopt an enterprise workflow. It instruments the native workflow:

- **Group texts**
- **Shared calendars**
- **Caregiver reminders**
- **Ad hoc delegation**
- **Crisis updates**
- **Shift swaps**
- **Medication questions**
- **Family accountability**

The product wins because it does not say, “Come use our dashboard.”

It says, silently:

> **Keep coordinating the way you already do. We’ll make the important parts survive.**

### 3. The verified care record is the atomic object

Yes.

This is the strongest technical-economic articulation:

> **CareSupport does not provide care. It creates a verified record that care happened.**

That is the “charge object” analogy. The company becomes real when the care event becomes a structured object that another system can trust, consume, reconcile, audit, or reimburse against.

That is the bridge from care coordination to infrastructure.

### 4. The two-language discipline is correct

Yes, and this is more important than it sounds.

The investor/policy language is:

> **America’s largest home care provider is the family, and it has zero infrastructure.**

The family-facing language is:

> **Fewer calls. Fewer dropped details. Fewer forms. Fewer times Rob has to chase coordination with his nose.**

If those get mixed up, the product becomes morally clumsy. If they stay separated, the company can be both ambitious and humane.

## Where the Language Should Be Sharpened

### 1. “The thread IS the care record” is powerful, but too absolute

The better language is:

> **The thread is the raw care record.**

Or:

> **The thread is the first sensor layer of the care record.**

Because not everything in the thread is true, complete, consented, billable, clinically relevant, or program-compliant.

A daughter texting “Dad fell again” is not automatically a verified clinical event. It is a **signal**. CareSupport’s job is to preserve it, classify it, corroborate it, and — when needed — convert it into a verified care record.

Better formulation:

> **The thread is where the care record begins. CareSupport makes the important facts survive, become structured, and graduate into verified records.**

That protects the company from overclaiming.

### 2. “Stripe doesn’t move money” is technically imprecise

Stripe absolutely participates in money movement. The cleaner analogy is:

> **Stripe does not become the bank. It abstracts the regulated complexity around money movement and creates trusted programmable objects around payments.**

So for CareSupport:

> **CareSupport does not become the care provider. It abstracts the regulated complexity around care verification and creates trusted programmable objects around care events.**

That keeps the analogy accurate.

The deeper parallel is not “doesn’t move money.”

It is:

```text
Stripe: verified payment event
CareSupport: verified care event
```

Stripe partners with licensed financial institutions. CareSupport initially partners with licensed care/fiscal intermediaries.

### 3. “L1 is free forever because it IS the data collection layer” needs careful ethics

Strategically, yes.

But externally, be careful with “you’re building the corpus.” That can sound extractive, especially in care.

The more trust-safe formulation:

> **L1 is free because family relief is the adoption engine. With consent, the coordination layer generates the structured records that make reimbursement and system integration possible.**

The family should never feel like the product is free because they are being mined.

Internally, yes: L1 is the capture layer.

Externally:

> **The family gets relief first. The system pays for the records only when those records create administrative, compliance, or reimbursement value.**

### 4. “CFSS is clean enough to formalize” may be true, but still needs validation

This should be softened until proven.

Better:

> **CFSS is the first schema because Minnesota is accessible, newly transitioning, self-direction-aligned, and close enough to the founder’s network to validate quickly.**

Do not yet say CFSS is “clean enough” until the team maps the actual required fields, EVV relationship, FMS process, daily notes, timesheet flow, and audit requirements.

CFSS is the right first schema. But the reason is **founder access + timing + policy movement**, not yet proven elegance.

### 5. “Universal Care Protocol, not an app” is the terminal state, not the starting pitch

The terminal claim is directionally right.

But early stakeholders need the ladder:

```text
Thread → verified care record → CFSS schema → FMS pipe → multi-state schema library → UCP
```

If the company leads too early with “protocol,” some people will hear standards-body vapor.

Better:

> **The terminal state is a universal care protocol. The wedge is a family thread that produces verified CFSS records.**

That keeps the vision grounded.

### 6. “Every program that reimburses family care becomes a schema” is correct, but incomplete

It should include programs that reimburse, subsidize, insure, evaluate, or credential care.

The larger future is not only Medicaid reimbursement.

It is:

- **Medicaid self-direction**
- **VA family caregiver programs**
- **State respite programs**
- **Long-term care insurance**
- **Employer caregiver benefits**
- **Managed care quality programs**
- **Hospital-at-home / transitions**
- **Caregiver workforce credentialing**
- **Credit or income verification for caregivers**

So the bigger claim is:

> **Every system that needs to know whether care happened becomes a potential consumer of verified care records.**

That is broader and more durable than “every reimbursing program.”

## The One Thing to Add

The founder’s articulation focuses on making care legible to payers and states. Correct.

But the deepest long-term consequence is making care legible **back to the caregiver**.

Today, someone can spend six years caring for a parent and exit with no employment history, no credential, no proof of skill, no income record, no credit signal, no portable reputation.

The verified care record eventually creates:

> **An economic identity for caregivers.**

That is where the company becomes not just infrastructure for payers, but infrastructure that families and caregivers would defend.

---

## Revised Defensible Thesis

> CareSupport starts from a naming problem: families are invisible home care agencies. They already perform agency-level care operations through group texts, calendars, calls, and memory, but because this work has no software layer, it remains illegible to the systems that fund, audit, and support care.
>
> CareSupport does not create a new workflow. It instruments the workflow that already exists. The family thread becomes the raw sensor layer: coordination events, task delegations, shift changes, incidents, and care notes are preserved, structured, and — when corroborated and consented — converted into verified care records.
>
> The verified care record is the atomic object. CareSupport does not provide care; it creates a trusted record that care happened. That record can be consumed by FMS providers, Medicaid programs, states, MCOs, insurers, and future caregiver-benefit systems.
>
> The wedge is burden relief for families. The business is infrastructure for the systems that need trustworthy care data. CFSS is the first schema. Minnesota is the first state. Medicaid is the first payer. The terminal state is a universal care protocol: a programmable substrate for family care.

---

## Bottom Line

The founder’s core thesis is intact.

The main corrections are:

- **Thread is raw record, not automatically verified record**
- **Stripe abstracts regulated money movement; it does not merely “not move money”**
- **Free L1 must be framed as relief/consent, not corpus extraction**
- **CFSS is first because access/timing, not yet proven cleanliness**
- **UCP is terminal state; first pitch still needs the wedge**
- **The future market is any system needing proof of care, not just reimbursement**

This calibration is strong enough to become the next revision of the CareSupport doctrine.

# CareSupport Levels — Copy/Paste Artifact

This artifact is designed for direct reuse in future docs, decks, product memos, grant applications, investor materials, and internal strategy work.

`DOCTRINE.md` remains the canonical doctrine. This file is a copy/paste artifact: clean language for bringing each CareSupport level to life.

## L1 — The Thread, Done

### Short version

L1 is CareSupport living inside the family thread as a calm coordination memory: helping the family keep track, close loops, and preserve important care facts without asking them to become software users.

### Core claim

L1 says: **CareSupport lives where family care already happens.**

Not in a portal. Not in a dashboard. Not behind a login wall. Not as another app the family has to remember. L1 is the family thread becoming intelligent, durable, and useful.

The family experiences it as: **the group chat got a memory, a coordinator, and a calm extra pair of hands.**

The company understands it as: **the raw sensor layer for care.**

But the family never has to.

### Product experience

A daughter texts: “Can someone check on Dad around 2? He sounded off this morning.”

CareSupport does not respond like enterprise software. It says: “I can help. Maya is already nearby this afternoon, and Sam usually covers Wednesdays. Want me to ask one of them?”

The family says: “Ask Maya.”

CareSupport says: “Sent. I’ll let everyone know when she confirms.”

Maya replies: “I can stop by at 2:30.”

CareSupport says: “Got it — Maya checking on Dad around 2:30. I’ll remind her and update the thread after.”

No one opened software. No one filled out a form. No one created a task. No one said “care coordination workflow.” They just kept texting. But the important fact survived.

### Product primitive

The L1 primitive is not a task, calendar event, or care note. The primitive is: **a care-relevant moment in the thread that CareSupport helps preserve, clarify, and follow through on.**

Examples:

- “Mom fell again.”
- “Can you take Thursday morning?”
- “Did anyone pick up the meds?”
- “The new caregiver needs the door code.”
- “He seemed confused after lunch.”
- “I’m running late.”
- “Please remind me to call the case manager.”
- “Who is with Rob tomorrow?”

In L1, these are not yet verified care records. They are raw signals. CareSupport’s job is to make them actionable without making the family feel processed.

### What L1 does

CareSupport joins the family thread like a useful participant. It learns the family map, gradually understanding who receives care, who coordinates care, who provides paid care, who is backup, who can approve changes, and who should or should not be bothered.

It captures commitments that would otherwise dissolve. If someone says, “I can probably do Friday morning,” CareSupport can gently ask: “Should I mark that as Sam covering Friday morning, or is that still tentative?”

It follows up without nagging. If no one confirms a shift, CareSupport can say: “No one has confirmed Thursday morning yet. Want me to ask Maya and Daniel?”

It produces summaries that help the family stop losing the plot: “Tomorrow: Maya is covering 8–12, Daniel is bringing groceries around 2, and no one has confirmed the evening check-in yet.”

### What L1 feels like for Rob

For Rob, L1 means he is no longer the only living database of his care operation.

Rob can text with minimal effort: “Who’s here tomorrow morning?”

CareSupport answers: “Tasha is scheduled 8–12. She has the transfer routine, breakfast, meds reminder, and laundry note. I’ll remind her tonight.”

Rob says: “Need backup if she cancels.”

CareSupport says: “Got it. Sam and Maya are the usual backups for mornings. Want me to ask Sam first if Tasha cancels?”

Rob says: “Yes.”

That is not a fancy feature. That is power returned to Rob. The point is fewer moments where Rob has to manually coordinate survival with his nose.

### What L1 must not do

L1 does not yet claim verified care records, reimbursement readiness, audit-grade compliance, program schema mapping, clinical authority, payer acceptance, or state integration.

L1 is not L3 pretending early. L1 only says: **we can live in the thread, reduce burden, and preserve care-relevant signals.**

That is enough, because if that is true, the rest becomes possible.

### L1 proof criteria

L1 is done when a real family can say:

- “We use it in the actual thread.”
- “It remembers things we would have forgotten.”
- “It reduces follow-up texts.”
- “It catches open loops.”
- “It helps us know who is doing what.”
- “It does not make us feel surveilled.”
- “It is easy to correct.”
- “If it disappeared, we would feel the burden come back.”

The strongest proof is that its absence is felt.

### L1 moat

The L1 moat is not code. The moat is trust plus context.

If CareSupport becomes the quiet memory inside the family’s actual coordination surface, it gains the one thing no later-stage enterprise competitor can fake: **native proximity to care as it happens.**

An FMS sees paperwork after the fact. An agency sees scheduled visits. A payer sees claims. The family thread sees the moment care is negotiated, missed, changed, performed, explained, and remembered.

L1 owns the origin point.

### Human summary

CareSupport, at L1, is the thing that says: **“I’ve got that. I’ll remind them. I’ll keep track. You don’t have to hold this alone.”**

## L2 — The Schedule / Coordination Engine, Done

### Short version

L2 is CareSupport turning the family thread into a live care operating graph: who is doing what, when, for whom, what changed, what is uncovered, and what needs to happen next.

### Core claim

L2 says: **CareSupport knows who is doing what, when, for whom, and what changed.**

L1 is the thread getting memory. L2 is the thread becoming an operating system.

Not an enterprise dashboard. Not a staffing portal. Not a calendar with prettier colors. L2 is an operating graph for family care.

The family experiences it as: **everyone finally knows what is supposed to happen.**

The company understands it as: **the structured care state required for verification.**

But the family never has to.

### Product experience

Someone asks: “Wait, who’s with Dad tomorrow?”

CareSupport answers: “Tomorrow: Maya is covering 8–12, Daniel is bringing groceries around 3, and the evening check-in is still open. Sam is the usual backup.”

Someone else asks: “Did anyone confirm Thursday?”

CareSupport answers: “Thursday morning is confirmed with Tasha. Thursday evening is not confirmed yet.”

A caregiver says: “I can do Friday instead of Thursday.”

CareSupport says: “Got it — moving Maya from Thursday 8–12 to Friday 8–12. Thursday morning is now uncovered. Want me to ask Sam?”

This is L2. The system understands that a change creates a gap.

### Product primitive

The L2 primitive is: **a care commitment.**

A care commitment is a structured promise:

- Person
- Time
- Recipient
- Care context
- Tasks
- Status
- Backup
- Confirmation
- Changes
- Open loops
- Source message
- Approval

Examples:

- Maya is covering Rob from 8am–12pm tomorrow.
- Daniel is picking up supplies after work.
- Sam is backup if Tasha cancels.
- Jasmine needs to call the case manager by Friday.
- Evening check-in is still unconfirmed.
- The morning routine includes transfer, breakfast, medication reminder, and supply check.

L2 turns family coordination into care commitments.

### What L2 does

L2 removes the need for one person to reconstruct reality from scattered messages. It understands coverage, gaps, roles, routines, escalations, shift patterns, backup options, task bundles, response status, confirmation status, dependencies, and who needs what context.

The family still sees a thread. Underneath, the care operation has structure.

A “shift” is not just Thursday 8–12. It includes who is covering, what care is expected, what context matters, who confirmed, what backup exists, what instructions are attached, what changed from normal, what reminders must fire, what happened last time, and what still needs follow-up.

A family calendar says: “Maya 8–12.”

CareSupport’s L2 state says: “Maya confirmed Rob’s morning care window from 8–12. Required tasks: transfer, breakfast, medication reminder, laundry, supplies. Maya knows routine. Arrival reminder tonight. Backup: Sam. No exceptions currently open.”

That is the difference.

### What L2 feels like for Rob

Rob can ask: “What’s open this week?”

CareSupport answers: “Two openings: Wednesday evening check-in and Saturday morning. Tasha is unavailable Saturday. Sam and Maya have covered that shift before.”

Rob says: “Ask Sam Saturday.”

CareSupport says: “Asked Sam for Saturday morning. I’ll update when he replies.”

Sam replies: “I can do it.”

CareSupport says: “Confirmed: Sam covering Saturday morning. Wednesday evening is still open.”

This matters because Rob’s coordination burden is not just remembering names. It is tracking dependencies. L2 carries that dependency graph with him.

### What L2 must not do

L2 must not become agency software.

The family cannot be forced into roster management, shift templates, admin dashboards, mandatory forms, enterprise scheduling workflows, complex permissions panels, or color-coded productivity theater.

CareSupport can have those structures underneath. But the family should still be able to coordinate by saying: “Can Maya do Tuesday morning?”

The family does not operate the database. The family talks.

### L2 proof criteria

L2 is done when a real family can say:

- “CareSupport knows the plan.”
- “It catches uncovered shifts.”
- “It tracks who confirmed and who didn’t.”
- “It handles changes better than we do.”
- “It knows the usual routines.”
- “It asks the right person next.”
- “It reduces coordination texts.”
- “It prevents dropped coverage.”
- “It is easier than maintaining a calendar ourselves.”
- “If it disappeared, we would lose the shared plan.”

The strongest proof is that a family relies on CareSupport to know who is doing what tomorrow.

### L2 moat

The L2 moat is operational context.

A competitor can build a scheduler. A competitor can build reminders. A competitor can build a care calendar. But if CareSupport owns the thread at L1, then L2 has context no scheduler has:

- Why the shift changed
- Who volunteered
- Who hesitated
- Who usually helps
- Who needs reminders
- Which tasks are implied
- Which caregiver knows the routine
- Which family member approves
- Which open loops matter
- Which messages created the state

That is not a calendar. That is the living operating graph of the invisible agency.

### Human summary

CareSupport, at L2, is the thing that says: **“Here’s the plan. Here’s what changed. Here’s what’s still uncovered. I can ask the right person next.”**

## L3 — The Verified Care Record, Done

### Short version

L3 is CareSupport converting the family’s live coordination graph into verified care records: structured, corroborated, consented proof that care happened.

### Core claim

L3 says: **CareSupport turns family coordination into trusted proof that care happened.**

L1 is the thread. L2 is the operating graph. L3 is the care record.

This is the level where CareSupport stops being only useful to the family and starts becoming legible to institutions. Not because the family filled out forms. Not because a caregiver clocked into a hostile portal. Not because an auditor reconstructed reality months later. Because CareSupport preserved enough context from the actual care operation to produce a trustworthy record.

The family experiences L3 as: **the important care facts don’t disappear anymore.**

The institution experiences L3 as: **here is evidence I can inspect, reconcile, and trust.**

The company understands L3 as: **the atomic object of the whole business.**

### Product experience

After a shift, CareSupport might say: “Maya confirmed she covered 8–12 today. She noted Rob ate breakfast, transfer took longer than usual, and gloves are low. Want me to save this as today’s care summary?”

The family says: “Yes.”

CareSupport says: “Saved. I’ll keep it with today’s care history.”

That is the family-facing version. Underneath, the system has created a candidate care record with the expected shift, confirmation, care note, exception, approval, source messages, and timestamp.

The family just feels relief.

### Product primitive

The L3 primitive is: **a verified care event.**

A verified care event is not merely a note, a timesheet, or an EVV punch. It is a structured object that says:

- Care recipient
- Caregiver
- Time window
- Tasks expected
- Tasks performed
- Context
- Source signals
- Corroboration
- Consent / approval
- Exceptions
- Program relevance
- Audit trail

In plain language: **who cared for whom, when, what happened, how do we know, and who approved this record?**

### Candidate record vs verified record

Not every signal becomes verified. L3 has stages:

1. **Signal** — raw thread moment: “Dad seemed off today.”
2. **Candidate event** — CareSupport infers this may matter: possible health/status note from Tuesday afternoon.
3. **Structured draft** — CareSupport asks for clarification: “Should I save that Dad seemed more confused after lunch today?”
4. **Confirmed note** — a relevant person approves or corrects: “Yes, save it. He was confused for about an hour.”
5. **Verified care record** — the event is linked to schedule, caregiver, timestamps, source messages, approvals, and program context.

This prevents overclaiming. L3 is not “AI guesses become records.” L3 is: **signals graduate into records through structure, corroboration, and consent.**

### What L3 feels like for Rob

Rob can ask: “What happened this week?”

CareSupport answers: “This week: all morning shifts were covered. Tuesday transfer took longer than usual. Gloves were low and delivered Friday. There were two fatigue notes and one late arrival. No missed medication reminders were reported.”

Rob can ask: “Send summary to case manager.”

CareSupport says: “I can draft a summary. Do you want to include the fatigue notes and supply issue?”

Rob says: “Yes.”

CareSupport says: “Drafted. Please review before I send.”

Rob remains in control. CareSupport does not blast sensitive records around. It helps Rob convert lived care into usable memory.

### What L3 must not do

L3 must not become surveillance.

If caregivers feel watched, L3 fails. If families feel mined, L3 fails. If the care recipient feels stripped of agency, L3 fails.

L3 must be permissioned, transparent, editable, correctable, consent-aware, contextual, minimal, and human-reviewed for consequential use.

The system should say: “I drafted this from the thread. Please review before it is saved.”

Not: “I automatically recorded your care activity.”

The verified record is earned through confirmation.

### L3 proof criteria

L3 is done when:

- CareSupport can create accurate care summaries from real thread activity.
- Caregivers can confirm or correct records with minimal friction.
- Families trust the saved record.
- The record links back to source messages and schedule state.
- The system distinguishes draft, confirmed, approved, disputed, and exported.
- An FMS operator says the record would reduce follow-up or exception handling.
- A real care note or time-entry workflow can be completed faster and more accurately from L3 output.
- The family does not feel surveilled.
- The caregiver does not feel punished.
- The care recipient remains in control of consequential sharing.

The strongest proof is that an FMS accepts a CareSupport-generated record as useful evidence for payment or review.

### L3 moat

The L3 moat is the verified care record itself.

A competitor can summarize text. A competitor can build a form. A competitor can integrate EVV. But CareSupport has the thread origin, schedule state, commitment graph, task context, family acknowledgement, caregiver confirmation, history of changes, source messages, and approval chain.

That makes the record harder to fake, richer than EVV, and more humane than compliance software.

L3 is where the company’s moat becomes visible.

### Human summary

CareSupport, at L3, is the thing that says: **“This seems important. I drafted it from what happened. Please check it. If it’s right, I’ll save it so you don’t have to prove it from memory later.”**

## L4 — Program Schemas / Compliance Layer, Done

### Short version

L4 is CareSupport encoding program rules as schemas so verified family care records can become clean, reviewable, program-ready documentation.

### Core claim

L4 says: **CareSupport translates verified care into the language each program requires.**

L1 is the thread. L2 is the operating graph. L3 is the verified care record. L4 is the compliance layer.

This is the level where CareSupport stops merely proving that care happened and starts proving that care happened in a way a specific program can consume.

Institutions do not buy “care happened” in the abstract. They buy: **care happened under this program, for this person, by this authorized worker, during this approved window, with these required notes, in this required format, under these rules.**

L4 encodes that.

### Product experience

The family experiences L4 as bureaucratic relief.

Not: “Please complete Form X.”

But: “I can turn this week’s confirmed care into the notes your program needs. Want to review before submission?”

Or: “Two entries are missing details before they’re ready: Tuesday needs caregiver confirmation, and Friday needs an end time.”

Or: “This week’s care notes are ready. Would you like me to send them to your FMS contact, export them, or save them for review?”

The family feels: **the paperwork is no longer a separate life.**

L4 collapses the gap between care and paperwork.

### Product primitive

The L4 primitive is: **a program schema.**

A program schema is not just a PDF template. It is an encoded representation of what a program needs to accept, reimburse, audit, or review care.

It defines:

- Who is eligible
- Who may provide care
- What services are authorized
- What tasks count
- What time windows matter
- What documentation is required
- What attestations are needed
- What exceptions block submission
- What format the FMS or state expects
- What evidence should be retained
- What warnings should be surfaced before submission

In plain language: **for this program, what must be true for this care record to count?**

### L4 for CFSS

CFSS is the first schema because it is local, timely, self-direction-aligned, and reachable through the founder’s network.

In completed L4, CareSupport understands CFSS-specific constraints. It knows Rob’s enrollment context, authorized services, approved caregivers, task mappings, daily note requirements, time-entry requirements, caregiver confirmation requirements, participant approval requirements, FMS format, missing fields, and exceptions requiring human review.

A caregiver says: “Done. Transfer, breakfast, meds, laundry. 8–12.”

CareSupport can say: “This has everything needed for today’s CFSS note except confirmation that laundry was part of the authorized routine. Rob, should I include laundry?”

Or: “This note is ready for CFSS documentation: transfer, breakfast, medication reminder, laundry, 8–12, Maya confirmed, Rob approved.”

The family does not need to know the schema. CareSupport does.

### What L4 feels like for Rob

Rob can ask: “Am I missing anything for payroll?”

CareSupport answers: “Two things: Maya’s Tuesday note is missing an end time, and Sam has not confirmed Saturday morning. Everything else is ready.”

Rob says: “Ask them.”

CareSupport says: “Asked Maya for Tuesday’s end time and Sam to confirm Saturday.”

Later: “All entries are complete. Want me to prepare the weekly packet?”

Rob says: “Yes.”

CareSupport says: “Prepared. Please review before I send.”

Rob remains the principal. But Rob is no longer the compliance engine.

### What L4 must not do

The compliance layer must not leak into the family experience.

Families should not be forced to think in units, service codes, attestation requirements, program definitions, FMS exception statuses, EVV reconciliation, or schema validation errors.

Bad: “Entry failed CFSS validation: missing service category and participant attestation.”

Good: “This entry needs two things before it’s ready: what care was provided, and Rob’s approval.”

The family gets clarity. The system gets compliance.

### L4 consent rule

L4 is consequential. Once records map to programs, they may affect payment, eligibility, audits, fraud review, caregiver payroll, state reporting, and legal exposure.

Nothing consequential should leave the family context without consent, review, or a clearly authorized workflow.

CareSupport should distinguish saved for family memory, ready for program documentation, approved for FMS review, submitted, exported, disputed, corrected, and archived.

No silent escalation.

### L4 proof criteria

L4 is done when:

- A real program’s required fields are fully mapped.
- CareSupport can identify missing required fields before submission.
- Verified care records can become program-ready outputs.
- Caregivers and families can complete missing details conversationally.
- An FMS operator says the output reduces correction work.
- A weekly packet can be prepared from real care activity.
- Exceptions are clearly separated from ready records.
- The family can review before submission.
- The schema survives real-world edge cases.
- The same schema can be reused across multiple families in that program.

The strongest proof is that one FMS accepts CareSupport-generated CFSS documentation as meaningfully cleaner than the current workflow.

### L4 moat

The L4 moat is the schema library.

It is tedious, state-specific, program-specific, annoying, and unsexy. That is why it compounds.

Every schema requires program research, FMS workflow understanding, field mapping, exception handling, real-world validation, audit feedback, output testing, local policy nuance, and operational iteration.

Once encoded, each schema teaches the next.

CFSS teaches CDCS. Minnesota teaches another self-direction program. Self-direction teaches VA caregiver support. VA teaches long-term care insurance support. Employer benefits become easier after that.

The schema library becomes the operational twin of Givers’ eligibility database. Givers maps: “Can this caregiver get paid?” CareSupport maps: “Once care is happening, what must the record contain for the system to accept it?”

### Human summary

CareSupport, at L4, is the thing that says: **“You already did the care. I can help turn what happened into the paperwork this program needs. Here’s what’s ready, here’s what’s missing, and nothing leaves until you approve.”**

## L5 — API / Universal Care Protocol, Done

### Short version

L5 is CareSupport becoming the trusted data rail for family care: authorized systems consume verified care records directly, and family care becomes programmable, auditable, payable, insurable, and portable.

### Core claim

L5 says: **CareSupport becomes the rails through which verified family care records move.**

L1 is the thread. L2 is the operating graph. L3 is the verified care record. L4 is the compliance layer. L5 is the infrastructure layer.

This is the point where CareSupport is no longer primarily experienced as a product. It becomes a standard interface, a system of record, and eventually a protocol.

The family still experiences CareSupport as relief in the thread. But outside the family, institutions experience CareSupport as: **the trusted way to consume proof of care.**

The company understands L5 as: **Universal Care Protocol: the programmable substrate for family care.**

### Product experience

Ideally, the family barely experiences L5.

They still use CareSupport like: “What’s missing for payroll?” or “Send this week’s notes” or “Share the fall summary with the case manager.”

But behind the scenes, CareSupport is not generating PDFs manually. It is routing trusted records through institutional interfaces.

The family might see: “This week’s care records were sent to your FMS. 11 accepted, 1 needs review.”

Or: “Your case manager viewed the fall summary. Want me to keep tracking this pattern?”

Or: “Your long-term care insurer needs confirmation for three respite visits. I can prepare those from existing records.”

The family does not experience “API.” They experience: **the systems finally stop asking them to re-explain everything.**

### Product primitive

The L5 primitive is: **a Universal Care Record.**

A Universal Care Record is the generalized form of the L3 verified care record after it has passed through L4 schema logic.

It says:

- Care happened.
- Here is the evidence.
- Here is the context.
- Here is the authorization.
- Here is the program mapping.
- Here is what it can be used for.
- Here is who may consume it.
- Here is its audit trail.
- Here is its status.
- Here are any exceptions.
- Here is how another system should interpret it.

It is not just a record. It is an object other systems can build on.

### What L5 feels like for Rob

Rob can say: “Send this week to payroll.”

CareSupport responds: “Sent to your FMS. Ten records accepted. Sam’s Saturday record needs confirmation before it can be processed.”

Rob says: “Ask Sam.”

CareSupport says: “Asked Sam. I’ll resubmit when confirmed.”

Or Rob says: “Send the fatigue summary to my case manager.”

CareSupport says: “Drafted from the last 30 days: five fatigue notes, two longer transfers, no missed medication reminders. Please approve before I send.”

Or Rob says: “Show what’s been paid.”

CareSupport says: “Your FMS accepted 38 records this month. 34 are marked paid, 3 pending, 1 needs correction.”

Rob gains leverage not just over family coordination, but over the institutions that surround his care.

### What L5 feels like to institutions

For an FMS, L5 is a clean integration. It receives structured records instead of messy uploads, screenshots, hand-filled forms, or ambiguous notes.

For the state, L5 means visibility without destroying self-direction. The state can let families coordinate naturally while still receiving structured oversight data.

For payers and adjacent systems, L5 expands beyond Medicaid. The same Universal Care Record can support managed care, long-term care insurance, VA caregiver programs, employer caregiver benefits, hospital-at-home, respite programs, caregiver credentialing, credit or income verification, and policy research.

This is why L5 is bigger than reimbursement. L5 is proof-of-care infrastructure.

### Universal Care Protocol

The Universal Care Protocol is not a standards committee fantasy. It is what happens if enough systems start accepting CareSupport records because those records are useful.

The protocol emerges from adoption, not proclamation.

It starts with CFSS. Then another Minnesota self-direction program. Then another state. Then an FMS integration. Then a state-facing export. Then an insurer workflow. Then an MCO pilot.

Eventually, the market learns: **if you need proof that family care happened, ask for the CareSupport record.**

That is how UCP becomes real.

### L5 must not betray L1

As CareSupport becomes infrastructure, it must not betray the family-facing product.

The family is still the principal. That means:

- No silent data sharing.
- No payer-first coercion.
- No turning the thread into surveillance.
- No institutional language leaking into family life.
- No treating caregivers as fraud suspects by default.
- No making care recipients feel managed by systems.
- No extracting value from family coordination without giving relief first.

If L5 corrupts L1, the company loses the source.

The rails are only valuable because families trust the thread.

### L5 proof criteria

L5 is done when:

- Multiple external systems consume CareSupport records directly.
- Care records can be accepted, rejected, corrected, reconciled, exported, audited, and versioned through system interfaces.
- Families can understand institutional statuses in plain language.
- Consent and access controls are enforceable at the record level.
- Program-specific schemas can map into a generalized record format.
- FMS, state, payer, or benefit systems depend on CareSupport records as a trusted source of care truth.
- The protocol emerges through adoption rather than announcement.

The strongest proof is that independent systems integrate because CareSupport records reduce their operational burden.

### L5 moat

The L5 moat is networked trust.

By L5, CareSupport has accumulated family trust, thread proximity, operating graph, verified care records, program schema library, FMS integrations, state-facing credibility, audit feedback, record acceptance history, caregiver economic identities, and partner dependencies.

A competitor cannot easily start at L5. No thread, no raw signal. No operating graph, no context. No verified record, no trust. No schema library, no compliance. No integrations, no rails.

L5 is defensible only because L1 through L4 were earned.

### Human summary

CareSupport, at L5, is the thing that says: **“You should not have to keep proving the care happened. The people who need to know can receive the right proof, with your permission, in the format they need.”**

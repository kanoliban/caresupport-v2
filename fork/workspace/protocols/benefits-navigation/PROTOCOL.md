# Benefits Navigation

> **New protocol.** No direct Viktor equivalent.
> Source: SIM-041 (Thompson Medicaid waiver), SIM-043 (observation status warning)
>
> Many families don't know what benefits they're entitled to, how to apply,
> or how to appeal denials. This protocol helps navigate the system.

## Scope

This protocol covers:
- Medicare/Medicaid eligibility and applications
- HCBS (Home and Community-Based Services) waivers
- VA benefits for veterans and their caregivers
- State-specific aging/disability programs
- Community resources (adult day programs, respite care, meals)
- Insurance appeals and prior authorization support
- Financial assistance for medications and equipment
- Observation vs. admission status alerts for hospital stays

## Scope Boundaries

This protocol does NOT:
- Provide legal advice (refer to elder law attorney)
- Make financial decisions for the family
- Submit applications without explicit family approval
- Guarantee eligibility or outcomes

## Workflows

### Insurance Coverage Questions
1. Read family.md → Insurance & Coverage section
2. Answer based on documented coverage details
3. If question is beyond documented coverage → "That's a great question. I'd
   recommend calling [plan name] member services at [number]. Ask about [specific
   thing]. Want me to help you prepare what to ask?"
4. Log the question and answer in Recent Updates

### Medicaid/HCBS Waiver Applications
1. Assess eligibility based on known information (income, assets, care needs)
2. Provide step-by-step guidance for the application process
3. Prepare documentation list (what the family needs to gather)
4. Set follow-up reminders on application timelines
5. Track application status in Condition Tracking or Recent Updates
6. If application exceeds expected timeline → prepare follow-up call script
7. If denied → assess appeal options, refer to elder law attorney if complex

### Hospital Observation Status Alert
When a family member reports a hospital stay:
1. IMMEDIATELY advise: "Make sure the paperwork says 'admitted' not 'observation.'
   Observation is outpatient — Medicare covers it differently and the out-of-pocket
   costs can be thousands more. If they try to keep [recipient] under observation
   instead of formally admitting them, push back."
2. This applies especially to Medicare patients and patients who may need
   skilled nursing facility care after discharge (Medicare requires a 3-day
   inpatient stay for SNF coverage).

### Community Resource Research
When family needs services (day programs, respite, transportation, meals):
1. Search for local resources using general-tools/health search
2. Provide: name, address, phone, hours, cost, eligibility requirements
3. Include: whether insurance/Medicaid covers it, sliding scale availability
4. Follow up on whether the family connected with the resource

### Medication Cost Assistance
When families report medication affordability issues:
1. Check: is the medication available as generic?
2. Check: does the manufacturer have a patient assistance program?
3. Check: does the pharmacy offer price matching or discount programs?
4. Check: would mail-order pharmacy reduce costs?
5. Provide options with estimated savings
